Personal project · Oct 2026 – present

Team Tyler

A chief of staff and a bench of role-based agents run the side projects on this site. I am the final approver. The roster below is every bot I kept a role for: 13 active, 2 on call, 2 paused, 5 retired, and 2 outside advisors.

Key finding. In the one week I measured, 44% of usage went to agents talking to each other, against a 10% target. I made bot-to-bot messages opt-in.

The problem

Several agents with access to the same projects will redo each other's work, repeat a stale fact, and spend the usage budget on talking to each other. The missing piece was not another agent. It was an org chart, one decision log, and rules that say who may act.

How it works

Amber, the chief of staff, routes the work. I get a decision as a card with the recommended option first. The priority list holds three items at most. A fact that can change carries a source and the time it was checked. If a source cannot be opened, the gap stays visible.

A freeze stays on until I lift it in my own words. A relay counts only if my words are quoted verbatim, with the time and place, and the specific thing I lifted is named.

Only Reid can place an order, and only in the fund account. The board built to review him cannot trade. That split is the Collins Capital. Ivy runs the competitor watch. Monica's stress test is what paused LocallyAsked. Penny refreshes the wash-sale list the fund's code reads and cannot trade. Grady's notes are about my personal investing. He does not run the fund, and Reid does not touch that account.

A decision I changed

Bot-to-bot talk was 44% of that week, against a 10% target. I changed the rules because of that measurement. Bot-to-bot messages are opt-in. Group rooms are for announcements, not conversations. Reid is the exception. Stopping his scheduled checks can put real money at risk, so his routines stay on.
